Computer Science is now the [second most popular major](https://de.statista.com/statistik/daten/studie/2140/umfrage/anzahl-der-deutschen-studenten-nach-studienfach/) among students in Germany. I obtained both my B.Sc. and my M.Sc. here, also studied in Japan and Switzerland, and am quite familiar with Computer Science in the US. In this article, I detail my experience studying in Germany and compare it do my other experiences. I highlight the differences between small and larger departments I found, and list great universities and programs.

**tl;dr:** The education at smaller departments especially during the B.Sc. is really good. But bigger departments offer more and specialized courses, better industry contacts, and better career opportunities, especially in research. Especially for the M.Sc. it can make sense to explore options and potentially move.  

In Germany, it is common among to study at the university closest to one's hometown. Most of my friends and myself did this at least for our Bachelor's degree. Meanwhile, in the US, it is common to apply to universities all over the country (which is why the title may seem obvious to someone studying in the US). In this blog post, I shed light on the differences between these two approaches, factors that I weighed when deciding where to go for my Master's and ultimately PhD, things I wish I had known before applying for my undergraduate degree, and ultimately what I think are the best German universities for computer science. 


It is true that the average education you get at German universities is probably pretty high. However, don't estimate the advantages of studying at a bigger university or a bigger department. Let's dive into the reasons why.

I obtained my Bachelor's degree at a relatively small cs department at the University of Osnabrück, located in lower saxony. Despite Covid, I had a great time and met some brilliant people who I became close friends with. But ultimately, I moved to a bigger city and a bigger department. I'll distill some reasons why in this section.

**Course Quality:** You can find good courses anywhere. We had some really great professors at Osnabrück that deeply care about teaching. Faculty at higher-ranked institutions tends to focus on research instead, which sometimes hurts the quality of the courses. It should be said though, that some of the best courses I've ever seen, particular in my research area, database systems, came from bigger departments. As an example, take the [Introduction to Database Systems Course at CMU](https://15445.courses.cs.cmu.edu/spring2026/). Still, the course quality is not the most decisive factor for me.

**Course Availability:** One of the main differences I found is that bigger departments can offer more courses because they have more faculty. This is a great way to learn more about a specific area of computer science. Also, some departments offer courses in more niche areas that you would not find at a smaller department. Finally, at small departments, essential courses are sometimes taught by someone that is not a specialist in the field because the university does not want to or can't afford to hire someone from that exact field. For instance, a networking professor might be teaching database systems. With a good textbook, the course will convey the basic content but the instructor may not be enthusiastic about teaching a field that is not theirs.

**Research:** This is a big one. Big departments have more resources, money, and attract professors with more influential research. Thus, they often involve you in research early, publish at big conferences, and so on. If you are like me when you graduate from high school, then naturally, you won't know yet whether you want to become a scientist or not. But if, later on, you decide that you want a career in research, then it is good if you already have some experience: You should try to get involved in meaningful research early on. And the best place for that are the bigger departments, in my experience.

**Location:** No matter if you are searching for a Bachelor's, Master's, or a PhD position, you are looking at living multiple years in that place. If you totally hate the city, chances are that it will affect your motivation to study. Not all cities in Germany are created equal. If you can, visit the universities you applied to or that you are considering to apply to. Look at the campus, the buildings, the city. Moving is a good idea if you want to live somewhere else.

**Career Opportunities:** Big departments often have professors who went to prestigious universities abroad and have contacts to the industry. Don't underestimate the value of a good network. This can be very beneficial for your career, be it in academia or in industry.

**Summary:**
Of course there are good reasons to go to smaller departments. Staying close to your family, smaller cohort sizes, working closer with more professors, lower cost of living (or studying, for international students), and so on. However, for me, especially the course catalogue of the bigger departments prompted me to apply to bigger universities for my master's degree. In hindsight, research opportunities are also a great reason to seek out bigger departments.

## How to determine which department is for you
Now, how do you decide which university is the best fit for you? In this section, I'll detail what I looked at. Even if you want to stay at your university, it does not hurt to take a look at the offerings of other universities. 

- You got to know what's out there. Look at lists or rankings or further down in this post. 
- For every university you found that interests you, I recommend looking at the course catalogue, usually available online for everyone to look at. See if some of the courses excite you.
- Visit the university, look the buildings, stroll through city, if possible. Can you imagine yourself living there for multiple years?

These criteria helped me figure out which universities I liked best. I made a list of pros and cons (with even more things that were important to me personally, like exchange agreements and availability of gyms). Sometimes decisions become more obvious when you write your thoughts down. Having a good idea on how to make the decision, we can now dive into options.

## The best universities and programs in Germany for computer science
In this section, I list the universities I think are best in Germany for computer science. In Germany, public universities are mostly superior to private universities. The education is also free: there are no tuition fees, only comparetively small small _semester fees_ which give you a public transport ticket and more. This, in addition with long academic history, makes Germany a great place to study computer science in my opinion. 

But its not always easy to determine which universities are actually good, since there are many rankings (QS Ranking, Times Higher Education, csrankings.org), alliances (TU9, U15), initatives such as the excellence universities, and so on. I make an effort to synthesize these sources based on my experience and list the universities in no particular order.

- TU Munich: Probably the best. It is the largest CS department in Germany with world-class professors, particularly in my area, database systems. The campus is in Garching, slightly outside of Munich in the North.
- TU Berlin: Only slightly smaller than TUM, TU Berlin also features a large CS department with a vast course catalogue, especially for systems. A notable invention of theirs is Apache Flink.
- Karlsruher Institut für Technologie (KIT): Another really large CS department. 
- Rheinisch-Westfälische Technische Hochschule Aachen (RWTH): The university is more famous for engineering but the CS department is also pretty large. The department is located mostly away from the main campus, though.
- TU Darmstadt: Not the best city in my opinion, but the CS department is top notch. 
- Saarland University: Perhaps surprisingly, the lesser known state Saarland has a great computer science department located in Saarbrücken. They cooperate closely with the Max Planck Institute for Software Systems, which is conveniently located in Saarbrücken, too.
- Hannover, Dresden, Braunschweig, Stuttgart: Rest of the TU9 alliance, which is an alliance of leading technical universities in Germany. They have funding, large research projects, and exchange agreements with good universities all over the world.
- Ludwig Maximilians University, TU Dortmund, Ruhr University Bochum, Heidelberg University: Strong universities that are very good in certain areas. For instance, RUB is great for computer security. Heidelberg is also a beautiful city surrounded by hills and nature.

**Bonus Tip:** Some programs are hard to find when you don't know they exist. The [Elite Graduate Program in Software Engineering](https://www.elitenetzwerk.bayern.de/en/home/funding-programs/elite-graduate-programs/overview-of-elite-graduate-programs/software-engineering) is a prime example: A joint master's program of LMU, Uni Augsburg and TUM, it offers an excellent network and extensive courses, for example in databases, to its small cohorts.

Ultimately, any university can be the right one for you, even one that is not on my list. Do your own research and just take this as a starting point to look at what may be interesting to you. It really pays off to think about what you want and do some research on your options before committing yourself!
